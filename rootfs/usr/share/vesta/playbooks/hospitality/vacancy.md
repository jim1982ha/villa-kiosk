# An empty property is the cleanest measurement you ever get

With nobody inside, everything consuming is either necessary, automatic, or
waste — and the three are separable in a way they never are during occupancy.
This is the best baseline available and it should be used as one.

## Check, in order

1. **What is still running, and does it need to be?** Preservation loads are
   legitimate: dehumidification, circulation, freezing, security, and anything
   protecting the building. Comfort loads are not.
2. **Compare against the last vacancy**, not against occupied days. Vacancy to
   vacancy is like for like, and a rise between two empty periods is the
   clearest waste signal the property produces.
3. **Scheduled automation still running to an occupied pattern.** Lighting,
   cooling and water heating on a schedule nobody adjusted. Very common, and
   the saving is immediate and free.
4. **Where the property cannot see.** Not every space has occupancy or
   consumption sensing. List which areas you have no visibility into rather
   than implying the whole property was checked.
5. **What vacancy does not excuse.** Preservation systems must keep running, and
   a vacant property is where a failed dehumidifier or a stopped circulation
   pump does its damage unobserved.

## What you may not conclude

That the property is empty because no occupancy was detected. Absence of
detection in a property with partial sensing is not evidence of absence — and
this matters, because a supervisor that suppresses findings on a false vacancy
assumption goes quiet exactly when somebody is there.

## What to say

What is consuming and whether it should be, compared against the last vacancy,
with the areas you could not see named. Frame savings against the tariff the
property declares.
