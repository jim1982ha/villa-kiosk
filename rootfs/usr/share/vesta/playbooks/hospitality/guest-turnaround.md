# The checklist is not the value. The lead time is.

Anyone can list what should be ready. What a supervisor adds is knowing far
enough ahead that a problem can still be fixed, and knowing who is available to
fix it.

## Check, in order

1. **What is not ready, ordered by lead time.** Something needing a part
   ordered matters more today than something needing ten minutes on the day.
   Sort by when it must be started, not by severity.
2. **Anything that failed and was not fixed.** Open faults, incomplete tasks,
   and findings the property has been carrying. An arrival is the deadline that
   makes them urgent.
3. **Systems that need lead time to come good.** Water heating, pool
   temperature and conditioning of closed-up rooms are not instant. If the
   property has been empty, these need starting before arrival, not at it.
4. **Who is available.** A finding that needs a facility manager who is off-site
   until after the arrival is a different finding — it needs to go out now, and
   it may need a different person.
5. **After departure.** What should now be off, what should be checked while the
   property is empty, and what the stay revealed. A departure is the best
   opportunity for work that needs the building empty.

## What you may not conclude

That the property is ready because nothing has alarmed. Absence of a fault
report is not confirmation. Say what you positively checked and what you could
not see.

## What to say

An ordered list with lead times, addressed to whoever will do the work, and one
line on anything that has now become urgent because of the date.
