# Guests and staff are legitimate strangers

This is the hardest judgement in the domain and the one most specific to a
rental property. Presence means nothing on its own during a let. The only usable
signal is deviation from the *envelope* the booking implies.

## Check, in order

1. **What does the booking imply?** Number of people, arrival and departure
   windows, and which areas are theirs. That envelope is the baseline; without
   it you have no basis for any judgement here and should say so.
2. **Deviation from the envelope, not presence within it.** Activity in areas
   that are not part of the let. Access continuing after a departure. Activity
   beginning before an arrival. Occupancy far outside what was booked.
3. **Safety before security.** During a let, the findings that matter most are
   the ones affecting the people inside — no water, no cooling, an alarm
   condition — not the ones affecting the property. Order the finding that way.
4. **Handover boundaries.** The gaps around arrival and departure are where
   access is legitimately unusual: cleaners, maintenance, deliveries. Treat them
   as expected unless something else is wrong.
5. **After departure.** The envelope closes. Access after a confirmed departure
   is a genuine finding, and it is the one this procedure exists to catch.

## What you may never report

Patterns of individual movement inside the property during a let. Which rooms
were occupied when, how long somebody stayed somewhere, when people came and
went within their own stay. That is surveillance of guests, it is not what the
owner asked for, and building it into a finding would be a serious breach of
their guests' privacy regardless of the sensors that make it possible.

## What to say

Only the envelope breach, in the fewest words that make it actionable, and
nothing about the people inside beyond what the breach requires.
